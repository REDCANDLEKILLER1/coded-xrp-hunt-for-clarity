# Fighter arrival, materials and authored deck

This remains a production checkpoint, not completion of the connected chapter or final realistic art.

The new `?review=landing` route runs an 18-second guided recovery sequence with the selected personal fighter. The same craft appears parked in the arrival bay with its canopy open, and XRPMan starts at its actual Pilot_Exit node. All three existing fighter identities have original Blender derivatives. `fighter=player|xrpl_striker|ledger_warden` selects the craft only on a fresh landing test save. A bounded `run` suffix creates an isolated repeatable section save.

The Warship uses v03 LOD0 geometry with a generated 1024-pixel armor albedo, original 512-pixel micro-normal map and packed roughness/metal map. Original anatomy/reference art is not used as a hull texture. The initial material export exposed an exporter defect: embedded images also carried external URIs, producing a white browser hull. Corrected exports contain only embedded images; runtime loading and validators now reject missing or external textures. The private failed study remains preserved as history.

Closed hull: 1,726,968 bytes, 19,250 triangles, six surfaces, three maps and all nine nodes. Open recovery derivative: 1,254,520 bytes, 19,326 triangles with the same node/map contract. The 8.8 x 12 m ventral opening is actual geometry. 1,458 swept samples cover all three fighter bounding volumes through the approach and vertical recovery legs. The full-detail UV geometry and three maps justify a measured <2 MB closed-hull budget; the earlier <500 KB target belonged to the untextured LOD2 review.

Authored deck: 4,046,020 bytes, 48,924 triangles, 32 surfaces, eight room nodes and three embedded maps. Plates, pressure floor, wall insets, ribs and service channels follow the same metre layout as gameplay. Checks sample 648 floor positions and all seven open navigation doorways. Narrow plate seams have a continuous pressure floor beneath them. Existing structural-shell checks still sample 648 positions enclosing the deck and 3.2 m headroom. These are sampled checks, not full interior collision certification.

The largest boarding model set is 11,450,944 bytes. With a reserved 900 KB renderer it remains below 12 MiB. A live crew-room sample initially exceeded rendering targets at 112 calls / 263,974 submitted triangles; limiting architectural shadow casters reduced unnecessary work. A later captured-bridge sample is 59 calls / 195,994 submitted triangles, including shadow passes. Neither is a ten-minute performance distribution or actual-phone result.

Played evidence: portrait arrival reached docking and the on-foot scene with no page errors and retained the fighter/checkpoint. That first sequence used the earlier proxy exterior; corrected materials were separately inspected in the browser. The revised eight-room route was played through the Core, followed by a saved-checkpoint reload, shield crossing, bridge capture, both first purchases, outbound briefing and a second reload. A test-harness direct waypoint encountered the Core terminal; walking around its visible footprint resolved it. All eight quest steps, Mr Zamn recruitment, Ledger Shield, original fighter and purchases persisted. No gameplay state was injected through the console.

All 45 checks and the build passed at this checkpoint; six actual Blender export/re-import checks preserve bounds, node transforms, triangle counts and map embedding. Portrait/landscape touch evidence from the prior prototype remains useful but is not a substitute for a fresh final-device check. Normal 2D entry, actual mesh space departure, portal/Mars and final art refinement remain to be completed.
